Importing Indian Millets to North America (USA & Canada): FDA Regulations, Customs Clearance & Bulk Sourcing Guide 2026

Subashri M • August 26, 2026 • 0 views

Unlocking North American Import Quotas for Indian Supergrains

With plant-based and gluten-free dietary demand expanding rapidly across the United States and Canada, North America is becoming one of the most lucrative commercial destinations for Indian millet exports. Here is what importers need to know about regulatory compliance, FDA Prior Notice, CFIA guidelines, and customs tariffs.

North American food manufacturers, wholesale distributors, and specialty grocery brands are increasingly looking beyond traditional grains like wheat and corn. Indian millets — rich in fiber, plant protein, and naturally gluten-free — are rapidly filling store shelves from California to Ontario.

1. Key Customs Classifications (ITC-HS / HSN Codes)

Accurate tariff code declaration is critical to ensure smooth US Customs and Border Protection (CBP) and Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) clearance:

Millet Product / Variety Harmonized System (HSN Code) US Tariff Classification (HTS)
Finger Millet (Ragi) 1008.21 1008.21.00 (Seed/Grain)
Pearl, Foxtail, Kodo, Barnyard 1008.29 1008.29.00 (Other Millets)
Millet Flour / Processed Meal 1102.90 1102.90.80 (Cereal Flours)

2. US FDA Compliance Requirements for Importers

To import commercial shipments of Indian millets into the United States, foreign suppliers and US importers must adhere to strict US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) protocols:

  • FDA Facility Registration: The exporting facility in India must maintain valid FDA registration under the Food Safety Modernization Act (FSMA).
  • FDA Prior Notice: Electronic Prior Notice must be submitted to the FDA before the shipment arrives at US ports of entry (minimum 8 hours before arrival by sea).
  • FSVP Verification: US importers are legally required to establish a Foreign Supplier Verification Program (FSVP) ensuring Indian suppliers meet US food safety standards.

3. Canadian CFIA Sourcing Standards

For Canadian buyers, the Canadian Food Inspection Agency (CFIA) enforces the Safe Food for Canadians Regulations (SFCR). Indian millet shipments must be accompanied by APEDA Phytosanitary Certificates verifying freedom from agricultural pests and maximum residue limits (MRLs).
